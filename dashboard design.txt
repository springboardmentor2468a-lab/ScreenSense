1. Age Distribution (Top-left grid)
This section shows the ages of children in the dataset, from 8 to 18. Age 13 is highlighted because it has the largest group, so most of the insights are focused on this age.

2. Exceeded Recommended Limit (Pie chart)
This chart shows how many children exceed the recommended daily screen time. Almost 91% of kids go beyond the safe limit, while only 9% stay within it. This is a major concern.

3. Educational vs Recreational Ratio (Bottom-center metric)
Here we see the ratio of educational to recreational screen time. The average is 0.40, which means children spend much more time on entertainment compared to learning.

4. Total Daily Screen Time (Bottom-right metric)
This number shows the total daily screen time across all ages. It adds up to 1,940 hours, which highlights how much time kids are spending on screens overall.

5. Gender Comparison (Donut chart)
This chart compares boys and girls. Both genders are almost equal in number and in screen time. This shows that screen habits are common across both boys and girls.

6. Device Usage (Bar chart – Primary Device)
This chart shows which devices children use the most. Smartphones are the most popular, followed by TV, laptops, and tablets. This tells us that mobile phones are the main source of screen exposure.

7. Screen Time by Gender and Device (Scatter plot)
This plot shows how much time boys and girls spend on screens, broken down by device. Both genders spend around 2,000 hours, with boys slightly higher.

8. Urban vs Rural Device Use (Horizontal bar chart)
This chart shows device usage by location. Again, smartphones dominate, with 457 children using them, while tablets are the least used at 90.

9. Health Impacts by Device (Stacked bar chart)
This chart connects device usage with health impacts. Children using smartphones show the highest health issues, followed by laptops, tablets, and TVs. This highlights the link between screen habits and well-being.
