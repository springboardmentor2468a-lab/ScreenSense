ScreenSense: Kids’ Screen Time Analysis (Power BI Dashboard Outline)

Slide 1 — Title & Introduction

Title:
ScreenSense: Kids’ Screen Time Analysis

Dataset:
Indian Kids Screen Time Dataset
Total Records: ~9,700+ children
Age Range: 8–18 years

Objective:
Analyze how children use screens, which devices they prefer, and how screen usage relates to health impacts.

Goal:
Identify high-risk screen-time patterns and provide insights for parents, educators, and policymakers.

Slide 2 — The Problem (Why This Matters)

Start with a thought-provoking question

Question:
"How many hours per day do you think children spend on screens?"

Global recommendation:
World health organizations suggest 1–2 hours per day for children.

Dataset insight:
Average screen time in our data is around 4+ hours per day.

That is more than double the recommended limit.

This raises concerns about health impacts, learning balance, and digital habits.

Slide 3 — About the Dataset

Show the main dataset structure

Original columns:

Column	Description
Age	Age of the child
Gender	Male / Female
Avg_Daily_Screen_Time_hr	Average daily screen time
Primary_Device	Main device used
Exceeded_Recommended_Limit	Whether limit exceeded
Educational_to_Recreational_Ratio	Learning vs entertainment usage
Health_Impacts	Reported health issues
Urban_or_Rural	Location type

Explain preprocessing:

• Cleaned missing values
• Handled inconsistent categories
• Engineered multiple new analytical features

Slide 4 — Feature Engineering

Derived features created for analysis:

Age Band

Child (8–10)
Early Teen (11–13)
Teen (14–15)
Young Adult (16–18)

Learning Heavy User

Users whose educational ratio ≥ 0.5

Health Risk Flag

Screen time greater than 4 hours

User Type

Learning-Dominant vs Recreation-Dominant

These features helped segment children into meaningful behavioral groups.

Slide 7 — Screen Time by Age Band

Visualization: Bar Chart

Insight:

Screen time increases steadily with age.

Younger children spend less time, while older teens show the highest usage.

Critical transition point appears around early teenage years.

This stage may be important for early digital habit interventions.

Slide 8 — Device Usage Distribution

Visualization: Donut Chart

Devices used by children:

• Smartphones
• Television
• Laptops
• Tablets

Insight:

Smartphones dominate device usage.

Nearly half of children primarily use smartphones, indicating mobile devices are the main driver of screen exposure.

Slide 9 — Screen Time Limit Exceedance

Visualization: Clustered Column Chart

Compare:

Age Band vs % Exceeding Limit

Insight:

Even younger children exceed recommended limits.

The issue is not restricted to teenagers.

High exceedance rates appear across all age groups.

Slide 10 — Device vs Screen Time (Cohort Analysis)

Visualization: Heatmap / Matrix

Rows: Age Band
Columns: Device Type
Values: Average Screen Time

Insight:

Certain combinations stand out as high-risk cohorts.

Example:

Older teens using laptops show very high screen usage.

Heatmaps help identify which groups require targeted awareness programs.

Slide 11 — Educational vs Recreational Usage

Visualization: Stacked Bar Chart

Compare:

Age Band vs Screen Time Purpose

Insight:

A significant portion of screen time is recreational rather than educational.

Even among older students, entertainment usage dominates.

This suggests the need to encourage productive digital engagement.

Slide 12 — Health Impacts of Screen Usage

Visualization: Pie Chart

Reported impacts include:

• Eye Strain
• Poor Sleep
• Headaches
• No Impact

Insight:

Eye strain and poor sleep are among the most commonly reported issues.

This highlights the health consequences of prolonged screen exposure.

Slide 13 — Key Insights Summary

Major findings from the analysis:

• Majority of children exceed recommended screen time limits
• Screen time increases with age
• Smartphones are the dominant device
• Recreational usage outweighs educational usage
• Several health impacts are associated with high screen exposure

Slide 14 — Recommendations

For Parents

Monitor smartphone usage and establish screen-time boundaries early.

For Schools

Encourage educational screen activities and digital balance.

For Policymakers

Promote awareness campaigns on healthy screen habits among children.

Slide 15 — Conclusion

Digital technology plays a major role in children's lives.

However, balanced usage is necessary to prevent health and behavioral risks.

Data-driven insights like these can help guide responsible digital habits.
